# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Inspire-first explorers: people who have not yet decided to go to Australia, or have decided
but have no idea what "going to Australia" actually means. They arrive at night, on a laptop,
in a northern-hemisphere winter, browsing rather than booking. They are curious, literate,
and allergic to being sold to.

The job to be done is not "book a trip." It is **"help me understand what this continent
actually is, so I can want a specific piece of it."** Success looks like a visitor leaving
with the name of a region, a season, and one experience they can picture themselves inside —
not a cart.

## Product Purpose

Southern Light is a field guide to Australia: a photographic and editorial survey of the
continent, organised by landscape rather than by state borders or booking inventory. It
exists because every other Australia travel surface flattens a landmass the size of Europe
into three photographs and a price. This one gives the place its actual scale.

It does not transact. It hands the visitor off to operators and parks with enough knowledge
that they arrive prepared, in the right month, with the right expectations.

## Brand Personality

**Weathered. Immense. Exact.**

The voice of a field researcher who has spent twenty years out there and is precise because
precision is a form of respect — for the country and for the reader. Never breathless, never
"unforgettable adventure." Gives real distances, real months, real names, real risks. Warmth
comes from specificity, not adjectives.

Emotional goal: awe, then orientation. The visitor should feel the scale first and the
practicality second, in that order.

## Anti-references

- **Generic OTA / booking site.** No dense blue chrome, no price badges, no filter rails,
  no "23 people are viewing this."
- **Cliché tourism-board template.** No Opera-House-at-sunset hero, no three identical
  cards, no kangaroo emoji, no teal-and-orange gradient wash, no "Best Time To Visit:
  October–March."
- **Generic AI SaaS landing.** No cream/sand backgrounds, no tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows
  above every section, no gradient text, no hero-metric row, no glassmorphism, no
  01 / 02 / 03 section scaffolding.
- **Editorial-magazine cosplay.** No display-serif italic headlines with ruled columns and
  lowercase tracked metadata. That is a saturated lane and this is not a magazine.

## Design Principles

1. **The photograph is the design.** Imagery is the primary content, not decoration around
   copy. Where a photograph belongs, a photograph ships — never a coloured panel.
2. **Scale is the message.** Australia's defining quality is emptiness and distance. The
   layout should feel wide, unhurried, and under-filled rather than dense. Horizon-width
   typography, long scroll, one dominant idea per fold.
3. **Night and day are the two grounds.** Story and landscape live on basalt dark; planning
   and practical information live on saltpan light. The ground shift carries meaning, not
   ornament — it is the difference between looking and preparing.
4. **Precision over persuasion.** Real names, real months, real kilometres, real
   Aboriginal place names and seasons. Never a superlative where a number would do more work.
5. **Country has custodians.** First Nations knowledge is presented as authority, not
   decoration. Traditional names lead where they are the correct name; the six Bininj/Mungguy
   seasons replace the tourist calendar because they are simply more accurate.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1, verified per token pair, on both grounds.
- Full keyboard operability with visible focus rings on every interactive element; skip link
  to main content.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` collapses every reveal, parallax, and marquee to an
  instant or crossfade state. No content is gated behind a transition — everything renders
  visible without JavaScript.
- Text over photography always sits on an explicit scrim, never on the raw image.
- Meaning is never carried by colour alone; season/difficulty states carry a text label.
- Descriptive alt text written in brand voice, naming the actual place.
- Touch targets ≥ 44×44 px with ≥ 8 px separation.
